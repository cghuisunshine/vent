# Start With Why (Simon Sinek) — Practical Tutorial & Workshop

## What this is (and isn’t)
- This is an original tutorial inspired by themes in *Start With Why*. It is not a copy of the book.
- Use it to learn the core model, craft a usable “Why,” and apply it to messaging, leadership, and product decisions.

## Learning goals
### By the end, you can:
- Explain the **Golden Circle** model (**Why → How → What**) in plain language.
- Draft a clear, testable **Why statement** (purpose/cause/belief).
- Translate the Why into **How principles** (behaviors) and **What proof** (evidence).
- Build a message that attracts the right people (customers, teammates, partners) without overselling.

## The Golden Circle (the core model)
### Definitions
- **Why**: The cause, purpose, or belief that explains *why you exist* beyond making money.
- **How**: The principles or distinctive ways you bring the Why to life (your “rules of behavior”).
- **What**: The products/services/outputs you deliver (the tangible things people can buy or observe).

### A quick test
- If your “Why” can be copied by any competitor verbatim, it’s probably a **What** or generic value statement.
- If your “Why” is just “to be the best” or “to grow,” it’s probably a **goal**, not a purpose.

### What “start with Why” changes
- Instead of persuading with features first, you lead with meaning and then back it up with credible proof.
- This is not a “marketing trick”; it’s a **clarity tool**. If your Why is unclear, the message becomes hype.

## Why-first communication (without manipulation)
### The ethical line
- **Alignment**: “Here is what we believe; if you share it, you’ll likely value what we do.”
- **Manipulation**: “Here are tactics to trigger purchase regardless of fit.”

### Practical guidance
- Say your Why as a **belief statement**, not a brag.
- Show your How as **observable behaviors**, not adjectives.
- Demonstrate your What with **evidence** (results, demos, testimonials, transparency).

## From Why to action: a usable ladder
### Step 1 — Write the Why (purpose/cause/belief)
Use this structure:
- “We exist to **[contribution]** so that **[impact]** for **[people]**.”

Examples (generic template, not from the book):
- “We exist to help first-time managers lead with confidence so teams can do their best work.”
- “We exist to make personal finance less intimidating so people can build real freedom.”

### Step 2 — Define How principles (3–7 rules of behavior)
Good How principles are:
- **Actionable**: You can do/not do them today.
- **Tradeoff-based**: They imply what you refuse to do.
- **Auditable**: Others can observe them.

How examples:
- “Teach before we sell.”
- “Default to small experiments over big launches.”
- “Be clear even when it costs us a deal.”

### Step 3 — Choose What proof points (what we ship + why it’s credible)
List the “What” and the proof:
- Product/service: what it is
- Proof: the measurable outcome, demo, customer story, or transparent artifact
- Link back to Why: which belief it serves

## Workshop agenda (90 minutes)
### 0–10 min — Setup
- Choose a subject: *you*, a team, a product, a club, or a class project.
- Decide a target audience: who you want to attract (and who you’re not for).

### 10–30 min — Why discovery (guided prompts)
Answer in short bullets:
- What problem are you consistently drawn to solve?
- What “unfair advantage” do you have (experience, proximity, worldview)?
- What do you believe is broken about the status quo?
- What future would you be proud to contribute to?

### 30–55 min — Draft and sharpen the Why
- Draft 3 versions; pick the one that feels most specific and true.
- Replace vague words (“empower,” “innovate”) with concrete outcomes.
- Check it against the “copy test” and “goal vs purpose” test.

### 55–75 min — Build the ladder (How + What proof)
- Write 5 How principles as “We will…” / “We won’t…”
- List 3 What proof points you can show within 2 weeks.

### 75–90 min — Message rehearsal
Deliver a 45–60s message:
- Why (belief)
- How (principles)
- What (proof)
- Ask (what you want the audience to do next)

## Common pitfalls (and fixes)
### Pitfall: Confusing values with How principles
- Fix: rewrite values as behaviors (“We are transparent” → “We publish decisions and tradeoffs weekly”).

### Pitfall: A Why that’s too broad
- Fix: narrow the audience or the impact until it becomes distinctive.

### Pitfall: Using Why as a slogan only
- Fix: attach a “decision rule” to the Why (what you’ll stop doing if it conflicts).

### Pitfall: Over-claiming
- Fix: keep Why aspirational but grounded; add proof commitments (“We’ll publish our progress monthly”).

## Decision-making with Why (a simple rubric)
For any initiative, score 0–2 on each:
- **Why-fit**: Does it serve the belief/purpose?
- **How-fit**: Can we do it without breaking our principles?
- **Proof**: Can we show credible evidence within a reasonable time?
- **Tradeoffs**: What do we stop doing to make this real?

Total (0–8):
- 0–3: likely distraction
- 4–6: consider as experiment
- 7–8: prioritize

## Exercises (solo or team)
### Exercise 1 — The “Five Whys” of motivation (personal)
- Why does this work matter to you?
- Why does *that* matter?
- Repeat 5 times; summarize the pattern in one belief statement.

### Exercise 2 — Audience alignment
- Write 3 sentences that would repel the wrong audience (politely).
- Write 3 sentences that would attract the right audience (clearly).

### Exercise 3 — Proof plan
- Pick one What proof point you can produce in 7 days.
- Define: artifact, owner, deadline, and how you’ll measure credibility.

## Quiz (self-check)
1) What is the difference between a Why and a goal?
2) Give one example of a How principle that implies a tradeoff.
3) Why should proof points be fast to produce early on?
4) What happens when the What is strong but the Why is unclear?
5) Write a one-sentence Why using the provided template.

### Answer key (short)
1) A Why is purpose/belief; a goal is a desired outcome.
2) Example: “We won’t ship features without customer validation.”
3) Early proof reduces hype and increases trust.
4) The message becomes feature-pushing and competes on noise/price.
5) Any clear, specific statement that includes contribution, impact, and audience.
