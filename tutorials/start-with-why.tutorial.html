Learning goals

By the end, you can:

  • Explain the Golden Circle model (Why → How → What) in plain language.
  • Draft a clear, testable Why statement (purpose/cause/belief).
  • Translate the Why into How principles (behaviors) and What proof (evidence).
  • Build a message that attracts the right people without overselling.

The Golden Circle (core model)

Definitions

  • Why: the cause/purpose/belief that explains why you exist beyond making money.
  • How: the principles (rules of behavior) that bring the Why to life.
  • What: the products/services/outputs people can buy or observe.

Quick checks

  • If your “Why” can be copied by any competitor verbatim, it’s probably a What or a generic slogan.
  • If your “Why” is just “to be the best” or “to grow,” it’s a goal, not a purpose.

What changes when you start with Why

  • Lead with meaning, then back it up with credible proof.
  • Use Why as a clarity tool; if the Why is unclear, the message turns into hype.

Why-first communication (without manipulation)

The ethical line

  • Alignment: “Here is what we believe; if you share it, you’ll likely value what we do.”
  • Manipulation: “Here are tactics to trigger purchase regardless of fit.”

Practical guidance

  • State the Why as a belief, not a brag.
  • Show the How as observable behaviors, not adjectives.
  • Demonstrate the What with evidence (results, demos, testimonials, transparency).

From Why to action: a usable ladder

Step 1 — Write the Why

Use this structure:

  • “We exist to [contribution] so that [impact] for [people].”

Examples (original, generic):

  • “We exist to help first-time managers lead with confidence so teams can do their best work.”
  • “We exist to make personal finance less intimidating so people can build real freedom.”

Step 2 — Define How principles (3–7)

Good principles are actionable, tradeoff-based, and auditable:

  • Actionable: you can do/not do them today.
  • Tradeoff-based: they imply what you refuse to do.
  • Auditable: others can observe them.

Examples:

  • “Teach before we sell.”
  • “Default to small experiments over big launches.”
  • “Be clear even when it costs us a deal.”

Step 3 — Choose What proof points

  • What: product/service/output.
  • Proof: measurable outcome, demo, story, or transparent artifact.
  • Link: which part of the Why the proof supports.

Workshop agenda (90 minutes)

0–10 min — Setup

  • Pick a subject: you, a team, a product, a club, or a class project.
  • Pick a target audience: who you want to attract (and who you’re not for).

10–30 min — Why discovery prompts

  • What problem are you consistently drawn to solve?
  • What “unfair advantage” do you have (experience, proximity, worldview)?
  • What do you believe is broken about the status quo?
  • What future would you be proud to contribute to?

30–55 min — Draft and sharpen the Why

  • Draft 3 versions; pick the one that feels most specific and true.
  • Replace vague words (“empower,” “innovate”) with concrete outcomes.
  • Run the copy test and the goal vs purpose test.

55–75 min — Build the ladder (How + What proof)

  • Write 5 How principles as “We will…” / “We won’t…”.
  • List 3 proof points you can show within 2 weeks.

75–90 min — Message rehearsal

Deliver a 45–60s message:

  • Why (belief)
  • How (principles)
  • What (proof)
  • Ask (what you want the audience to do next)

Common pitfalls (and fixes)

Pitfall: values that aren’t behaviors

  • Fix: rewrite values as actions (“We are transparent” → “We publish decisions and tradeoffs weekly”).

Pitfall: a Why that’s too broad

  • Fix: narrow the audience or impact until it becomes distinctive.

Pitfall: Why as a slogan only

  • Fix: attach a decision rule (what you’ll stop doing if it conflicts).

Pitfall: over-claiming

  • Fix: keep Why aspirational but grounded; add proof commitments (e.g., publish progress monthly).

Decision-making rubric

Score 0–2 on each:

  • Why-fit: does it serve the belief/purpose?
  • How-fit: can we do it without breaking principles?
  • Proof: can we show credible evidence soon?
  • Tradeoffs: what do we stop doing to make it real?

Total (0–8):

  • 0–3: likely distraction
  • 4–6: consider as experiment
  • 7–8: prioritize

Exercises

Exercise 1 — “Five whys” of motivation (personal)

  • Why does this work matter to you?
  • Why does that matter?
  • Repeat 5 times; summarize the pattern as one belief statement.

Exercise 2 — Audience alignment

  • Write 3 sentences that politely repel the wrong audience.
  • Write 3 sentences that clearly attract the right audience.

Exercise 3 — Proof plan

  • Pick one proof point you can produce in 7 days.
  • Define: artifact, owner, deadline, and credibility measure.

Quiz (self-check)

  1. What is the difference between a Why and a goal?
  2. Give one example of a How principle that implies a tradeoff.
  3. Why should proof points be fast to produce early on?
  4. What happens when the What is strong but the Why is unclear?
  5. Write a one-sentence Why using the provided template.

Answer key (short)

  1. A Why is purpose/belief; a goal is a desired outcome.
  2. Example: “We won’t ship features without customer validation.”
  3. Early proof reduces hype and increases trust.
  4. The message becomes feature-pushing and competes on noise/price.
  5. Any clear, specific statement with contribution, impact, and audience.
